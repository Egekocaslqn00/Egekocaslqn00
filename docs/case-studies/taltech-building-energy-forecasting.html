Extreme Cold-Start Building-Energy Forecasting

How a cross-building transfer-learning approach and a 12-member ensemble were evaluated for ten months of hourly energy consumption using only two months of winter history.

Discuss a similar problemData drift is a separate topic

Context: Ege Koçaslan completed an academic research internship in Data Science & System Modeling at Tallinn University of Technology (TalTech), Tallinn, Estonia, from 24 August to 26 October 2026. This page summarizes the specific building-energy forecasting work described in the TalTech participation certificate and the signed recommendation letter.

Problem and constraints

The forecasting task was to estimate ten months of unseen hourly building-energy consumption with only two months of winter historical data. This is an extreme cold-start setting: limited target history makes seasonal pattern estimation and long-horizon evaluation especially important.

  • Target: hourly building-energy consumption.
  • Available target history: two winter months.
  • Forecast horizon: ten months of unseen hourly consumption.
  • Evaluation: a blind test described in the recommendation letter across 7,344 hourly observations.

Approach

  1. Ensemble: built a 12-member ensemble combining LightGBM, XGBoost, and temporal profiles.
  2. Cross-building information: used four adjacent buildings as virtual sensors to represent campus activity and applied cross-building transfer learning.
  3. Calendar and external context: integrated national-holiday and academic-calendar context, evaluated external variables, and developed a secondary forecasting model.
  4. Explainability and validation: applied feature engineering, SHAP-based explanations, robust cross-validation, and uncertainty analysis.
  5. Communication: prepared executive reporting, technical documentation, and reproducible research code.

Reported results

The participation certificate confirms a 12.42% MAPE on the actual electricity-load profiles of Building X. The recommendation letter reports a blind test across 7,344 hourly observations, with 12.42% MAPE and a 0.9456 correlation coefficient.

What this case does—and does not—show

This is a documented academic research case for cold-start building-energy forecasting and cross-building transfer learning. It demonstrates work in system modeling, time-series forecasting, feature engineering, model evaluation, SHAP explainability, and uncertainty analysis under limited data.

Scope and limits: This TalTech case is not a data-drift deployment, is not evidence that every cold-start problem can be solved, and does not claim delivery in two weeks. The internship ran from 24 August to 26 October 2026. Results on a new client dataset require a separate evaluation.
