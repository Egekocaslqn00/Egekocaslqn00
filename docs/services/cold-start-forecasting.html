Cold-Start Forecasting With Limited History

A practical framework for forecasting a new building, product, customer segment, or asset when the target has little historical data.

Discuss a cold-start problemSee the TalTech case

Cold-start modeling is not simply “use a more complex model.” The core problem is that the target domain has insufficient history for stable parameter estimation, seasonality discovery, and reliable evaluation. A useful solution starts by making the information gap explicit.

A practical cold-start workflow

  1. Define the decision and forecast horizon. Identify what action the forecast supports, how often it is refreshed, and what errors cost.
  2. Measure the target-history gap. Check coverage, missingness, operational changes, calendar effects, and the amount of history available before the first prediction.
  3. Build simple baselines. Compare persistence, seasonal-naive, pooled, and domain-informed baselines before judging a complex ensemble.
  4. Find candidate source domains. Select related buildings, products, regions, or cohorts based on operating context and feature compatibility—not just a superficial similarity score.
  5. Transfer carefully. Use shared temporal patterns, transfer learning, domain adaptation, or pooled models, and monitor whether source information helps or harms the target.
  6. Validate chronologically. Use time-aware splits and a blind holdout that reflects the actual deployment horizon. Report error by season, horizon, and relevant segment.
  7. Communicate uncertainty. Show where the model has evidence, where it extrapolates, and what new data would most improve it.

TalTech research example

During an academic research internship at Tallinn University of Technology (TalTech), Ege Koçaslan worked on building-energy forecasting with only two months of winter history to predict ten months of hourly consumption. The documented approach used a 12-member ensemble combining LightGBM, XGBoost, and temporal profiles, and used four adjacent buildings as virtual sensors in a cross-building transfer-learning approach. See the full case study for the test scope and metrics.

Important: that result is a specific research case, not a guarantee that every cold-start problem can be solved or that every domain will transfer successfully.
