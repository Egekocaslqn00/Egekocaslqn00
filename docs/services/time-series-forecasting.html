Time-Series Forecasting That Respects Time

Forecasting workflows for business data where seasonality, changing conditions, limited history, and future information leakage matter.

Discuss forecastingRead the building-energy case

Forecast quality depends as much on the evaluation design as on the model. Random train/test splits can leak future patterns into the past. A useful time-series workflow keeps the decision horizon, information availability, and business cost of errors visible.

Forecasting checklist

  1. Define the horizon and cadence. Specify whether the forecast is hourly, daily, or monthly and how far ahead it must reach.
  2. Audit timestamps and coverage. Check time zone, daylight-saving changes, duplicates, missing intervals, and target revisions.
  3. Establish temporal baselines. Include persistence and seasonal-naive comparisons appropriate to the data.
  4. Use chronological validation. Train on the past, validate on the future, and consider rolling-origin evaluation for multiple conditions.
  5. Match metrics to the decision. Report scale-sensitive and scale-free metrics where useful, and break errors down by horizon and segment.
  6. Control feature leakage. Ensure each feature would truly be available at the time of prediction.
  7. Communicate uncertainty. Avoid implying that a point estimate is certain; show intervals or scenario ranges when justified.
